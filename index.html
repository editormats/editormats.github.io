Behind the Work

How It's Made

Not captured — constructed. Every shot is deliberate.

01

Replay System

Every edit starts in Orion Drift's built-in replay viewer. I rewind, reposition, and reconstruct moments — treating each match as raw footage and the spectator camera as a lens I fully control.

02

Custom Animator Script

I built a Luau-based camera animator that runs inside the spectator system. Full keyframe control over position, rotation, FOV, and depth of field — with custom spline interpolation modes. No preset shots. Every movement is scripted.

03

ReShade

Before footage leaves the game it runs through a custom ReShade pipeline. Colour grading, ambient occlusion, bloom, and sharpening — all tuned per-shot, not per-preset.

04

After Effects

Compositing, motion tracking, keying, and VFX layering. This is where the cinematic layer gets built — environment blends, character integration, and effects that don't exist in-game.

05

Blender

3D integration when a shot demands it. Object animation, scene construction, or sequences that need to exist in three-dimensional space before compositing.

06

Final Cut

Colour grade, sound design, pacing. Time remapping is central here — every impact, drop, and transition is frame-accurately locked to the music. If a cut doesn't hit on the beat, it's wrong. 4K output, always.

About

The Editor

I'm Mats — a cinematic editor and top competitive Orion Drift player. I don't capture gameplay; I construct shots. Using a custom camera animator built inside the game's spectator system, I treat every replay like a director's cut.

The workflow runs from in-game camera scripting through a ReShade pipeline, into After Effects for compositing, and Blender when the shot needs a third dimension. I work at 4K. I notice when something's off. I don't ship until it looks right.

Competing for team COLD. Posting everything at @editormats.
